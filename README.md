There is no single OKX minimum deposit. Crypto transfers have a floor set per coin and per network, fiat purchases have a floor set by whichever payment provider processes the order, and the deposit screen shows the live figure for the exact combination you selected. The numbers you will actually meet: roughly 1 USDT on a TRC20 USDT transfer, about $30 to $50 through a third-party card or bank ramp, and around €50 for SEPA in the European Economic Area.

One rule applies to all of them, and it is the reason the number matters. OKX states that if a deposit is below the minimum displayed, "the funds will not be credited, nor will they be refunded." Underpaying does not produce a partial credit. It produces a loss.

## Why three different pages give you three different numbers

Search this term and you will find $1, $10, $30–$50 and €50 in the same afternoon. They are answering different questions.

**$1** comes from affiliate sites that state "the minimum deposit at OKX is $1" without naming a coin or a network. It is the kind of claim that cannot be checked, because the official help page never publishes a flat figure. The closest true statement is that some networks have minimums in the 1 USDT range, which is not the same thing.

**$10** is where several card ramps and P2P merchants set their floor. It describes a fiat purchase route, not a blockchain transfer.

**$30–$50** is Banxa's typical range on OKX, with US ACH and card deposits commonly starting at $50 and daily caps that depend on your account status and KYC level. **€50** is the SEPA figure reported for euro bank transfers, and the exact floor for a given country is set by the rail, so it moves.

TradingView's broker comparison lists OKX's minimum as a dash, which is the most honest third-party answer of the lot: not disclosed, because there is nothing single to disclose.

The pattern here matters more than any one number. OKX's help page explains how to find the minimum and gives no amounts at all, so every article quoting a hard figure is quoting a payment channel, a network, or a guess. Treat it accordingly.

## What happens if you send less than the minimum

Your deposit does not get topped up, held for later, or returned. It is not credited to your account.

Recovery exists in a narrow sense and is not something to plan around. Some coins can be retrieved after a failed or misdirected deposit, and the recovery itself carries a fee. Others cannot be retrieved at all, which is the usual outcome when the network was wrong rather than the amount.

## Three mistakes that turn a small deposit into a total loss

**Sending on the wrong network.** ERC-20 USDT sent to a TRC-20 address is normally gone. Before you send, confirm the network appears in OKX's deposit screen for that coin, then confirm the sending wallet or exchange supports the same one. USDT on BSC (BEP20) has not been on OKX's supported list, so it is worth checking both ends rather than assuming the cheapest chain works.

**Skipping the memo or tag.** XRP, EOS and a handful of others need a destination tag or memo alongside the address. A transfer without it can land in the platform's main wallet with no way to attribute it to you.

**Landing exactly on the minimum.** If you send precisely 1 USDT, the sending side's gas comes out first and the credited amount can fall below the threshold. Send a little above the floor, not exactly at it. The same logic applies to card purchases: a $200 buy at the top of Banxa's 4.99% range leaves roughly $190 of crypto, and that is before any bank fee.

## Two verifications that block deposits before they start

OKX requires identity verification before you can deposit or withdraw: a government-issued photo ID and usually a selfie. It typically clears in 5–15 minutes and can take up to 24 hours during heavy periods.

Banxa runs its own KYC separately, sometimes asking for proof of address on top of the ID. If a Banxa payment fails after the money left your account, refunds usually land within 2–7 business days. That is a different clock from an OKX support ticket, and the two do not track each other.

## Deposit stuck? Check in this order

Open a block explorer and confirm the transaction hash (TXID) has reached the required number of confirmations. If the chain shows it confirmed but OKX does not, compare the network and the memo or tag against what the deposit screen asked for. Once both of those check out and more than two hours have passed, contact OKX support through live chat with the TXID, the deposit address and the exact amount sent. Support can trace a transfer sitting in a queue; they cannot fix a wrong network.
